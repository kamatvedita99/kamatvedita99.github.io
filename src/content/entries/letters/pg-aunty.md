title: "To the PG aunty"
date: 2026-10-10
note: "Written on 10 October 2026. Never sent."
---

**Hi, PG Aunty,**

I never thought I would write a letter to you that I would never send. But here I am.

I know you never liked me. I know I'm messy sometimes. I don't always keep things perfectly clean. I know I have my flaws.

But I never hated you.

I ignored things. I adjusted. I managed. I stayed.

After years of living here, this place slowly became my PG home. Maybe it wasn't perfect, but it was familiar. It was where I came back after exhausting days. It was where I thought I could close my door, lie down on my bed and finally breathe.

You took that away from me.

It doesn't feel like home anymore. It doesn't feel like a safe space.

I feel homeless in Mumbai.

Coming back here started feeling like walking into a courtroom. I never knew when another accusatory message would come. I never knew what would be considered wrong next. You made me feel like I was constantly being watched, judged and found guilty.

You charged me unfairly because you didn't want me staying at home. But I **pay for this place.** Where exactly am I supposed to go?

You could have simply told me you didn't want me here. You could have asked me to leave. At least then I would have known where I stood.

Instead, you kept creating rules, scenes and accusations. You made my life miserable in a place where I was supposed to have some peace.

And what hurts the most is how easily you ask me for things when you need them.

The same person who can speak to me so harshly can suddenly ask me for a favour. You can treat me badly one day and then casually ask me to bring you something from Goa the next.

I genuinely don't understand how people do that.

I have never hated people easily. I am someone who finds something to love in almost everything. I try to understand people. I give people chances. I ignore things because I don't want unnecessary conflict.

But I hate what you have made me feel.

You have been toxic to me.

And maybe you don't even realize how deeply your behaviour affected me.

You sent photographs of clothes I had washed and tried, lying on my bed, as though I had committed some offence. And then the next day, you could ask me to bring you neerphanas from Goa.

You took pictures of the kitchen and questioned me about it even when everyone else thought it was fine.

You photographed my sanitary pads when they had been there for a few days and sent those pictures in the group, while other people could leave things for days without the same reaction.

When I stayed home and worked from home, you created an issue around that too.

I paid rent.

I was not occupying a free space in your house.

So where was I supposed to go?

I was working. I was exhausted. I was trying to build a life in a city away from home. And the one place where I should have been able to rest became another source of anxiety.

Maybe having power over someone feels good. Maybe it felt good to you to decide what was right and what was wrong, and to announce that I was wrong before even asking me what happened.

But you don't realize what that does to a person.

You don't realize what it feels like to spend an entire day working and then come home wondering what accusation might be waiting for you.

You don't realize what it does when your home stops feeling like somewhere you can relax.

You took something precious from me.

You took away the feeling that I had a home in Mumbai.

And I don't think I will ever forgive you for that.

Not because I expect you to care. You probably don't.

But because I know what this place meant to me.

People usually document their good memories. I document the bad ones too. Maybe because I never want to gaslight myself into thinking something didn't happen just because enough time has passed.

I know what I experienced here.

I know how many times I swallowed my anger.

I know how many times I chose silence because I didn't want another fight.

I know how many times I came back here already exhausted and somehow ended the day feeling even more drained.

And yes, somewhere deep inside me, I hope that one day you understand what your behaviour does to people.

I don't wish bad things for people. I genuinely don't.

But I do hope that someday you experience the consequences of treating someone the way you treated me. I hope you understand what it feels like when the place that is supposed to be your safe space doesn't feel safe anymore.

Because you broke something in me.

Not permanently. I know that.

But you broke my sense of peace in a place that was supposed to be home.

There are millions of people who come to Mumbai for work, exhausted after long days, just wanting to come back to their beds and feel okay for a few hours.

I was one of them.

And you made me realize that I had nowhere in this city where I could simply exist without being questioned.

My last few months in Mumbai are left.

I hope this chapter ends soon.

And I hope that after I leave, I never have to deal with you again.

Enemies are sometimes easier to understand than people like you. At least with an enemy, you know they want to hurt you. You can protect yourself.

But when someone plays with your emotions, constantly makes you feel like you're wrong, speaks harshly to you and then comes back asking you for favours as though nothing happened — that is much harder to understand.

Maybe I will never understand you.

Maybe you will never understand me.

That's okay.

I don't need you to understand me anymore.

I just needed somewhere to put all of this.

So this is where I'm putting it.

On paper.

Not in your WhatsApp.

Not in another argument.

Not in another explanation where I have to defend myself.

Here.

And then I'm going to leave it here.

Because you may have taken away the feeling of home from this place.

But you don't get to take away my ability to build one somewhere else.

One day, I will have a home where I don't have to feel watched.

Where I don't have to defend why I am there.

Where I can come back after a terrible day and simply close the door.

Where I can breathe.

And when I have that home, I will remember this place not as the place where you broke me, but as the place where I learned exactly what I will never tolerate again.

**I hope this chapter ends soon.**

**And I hope I never have to see you again.**
